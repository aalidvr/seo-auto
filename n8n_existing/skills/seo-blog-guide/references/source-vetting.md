# Source vetting for the E-E-A-T citation

The one external quote is a trust signal, so the source has to be one a careful editor would accept.
A source qualifies only if it passes ALL five gates. When in doubt, keep searching for a better one.

## The five gates

1. **Authoritative** — a university, science/education body, government agency, standards body, or a
   technical/industry organisation. Its job is knowledge, not selling.
2. **Relevant** — topically specialised to the claim. "Relevant" does NOT mean it must be in your
   exact niche (a rug article needn't cite a rug site); it means the page genuinely covers the
   underlying subject — e.g. fibre/material science, health, thermal physics, safety.
3. **Non-competitor** — never another shop, brand, or site that competes with the user's site for the
   same customers or keywords. Citing a competitor hands them a link and undercuts the page.
4. **Non-commercial** — not a store, not a brand promoting its own product, not an affiliate/SEO
   content farm. Marketing pages fail even when the parent org is well-known.
5. **Not Wikipedia** — Wikipedia (and other open wikis) don't count as an authoritative primary
   source here. Use what Wikipedia cites, not Wikipedia itself.

## Verify the quote, don't trust memory

Always `web_fetch` the page and copy the quote **verbatim** from the fetched text. Do not quote from a
search snippet, and never reconstruct a remembered quote — wording and punctuation must be exact.
Keep it ≤15 words; if the useful sentence is longer, quote a ≤15-word fragment and lead with an
ellipsis. One quote per source.

## Examples

| Source | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| University science-education hub, fibre-properties page | ✅ use | Authoritative, relevant, neutral |
| Government health/allergy page (dust mites, asthma) | ✅ use | Authoritative, relevant, non-commercial |
| Standards / industry-technical body page | ✅ use | Authoritative, specialised |
| A rug/furniture retailer's blog | ❌ skip | Competitor + commercial |
| A brand's own product page promoting its material | ❌ skip | Commercial marketing |
| Wikipedia article | ❌ skip | Not an accepted primary authority |
| Generic listicle / content farm | ❌ skip | Not authoritative |

## If nothing qualifies

Broaden the claim you're citing (e.g. cite the material-science fact rather than a product claim) and
search again. It's better to spend another search than to ship a weak or commercial citation — and
far better than inventing one. If, after genuine effort, no qualifying source exists for any claim in
the article, tell the user rather than fabricating.
